SUMMARY

The adventure began with a chill in the air at the village of Oakhurst. For years, a mysterious goblin party had arrived every summer and winter solstice to sell a single, disease-curing apple for 50 gold pieces, but this year, the goblins never came. Instead, stories filtered back of a missing band of local heroes who had ventured into the Sunless Citadel to solve the mystery.

Our party of five arrived with their own heavy burdens. Vlad Laurent carried a letter from House Hucrele, desperate for him to find the children, Sharwyn and Talgen Hucrele and his acquaintance, Sir Braford. Alejandro Perez and Lucas Aedansbane, an unlikely duo of a traveling healer and a pragmatic smuggler, were sent by Alejandro’s church to investigate the “miracle” fruit. Arthur Blackthorn followed the orders of his druidic circle to find a rogue druid named Belak and stop the apples from disrupting the natural balance. Finally, there was Draven Shadowveil, whose sickle which had been found when he was trying to escape prison had suddenly begun whispering three ominous words: “Wake it not”.

The group reached a massive ravine where a knotted rope dangled into the dark. After Arthur identified some suspiciously large rat tracks, the heavily armoured Vlad led the way down a zig-zagging, narrow staircase. His clanking plate mail made a stealthy entrance impossible, and a giant rat lunged from the shadows at the bottom. Vlad didn’t hesitate, drawing his great axe and cleaving the rodent in two with a single strike.

Pushing into a circular courtyard, the heroes found the scene of an old battle. Four goblins had been dead for months—one was even skewered to the wall by a spear. When Lucas pulled the spear free, he revealed a hidden Draconic inscription: “Ashardalon”. Vlad’s draconic heritage kicked in, recognizing the name of an ancient and powerful red dragon whose cult had once inhabited these ruins.

The ruins proved as treacherous as the stories suggested. Lucas spotted a faint crack on the floor and identified a pressure-plate pit trap. Vlad, perhaps feeling overconfident after his victory over the rat, decided to test the trap’s stability by stomping on it with one leg. The maneuver failed spectacularly; he lost his balance and tumbled eight feet down into a pit of spikes. A second giant rat scurried from the debris to finish him off, but Vlad dispatched it from the floor of the pit before the party hauled him back up with a rope. Lucas then showed off his professional touch, using his thieves’ tools to jam the trap shut permanently.

Moving deeper, the party entered a dark chamber and was met by the glowing red eyes of three skeletons. Lucas opened the fight by sliding his rapier into a skeleton’s eye socket, while Vlad shattered another with his axe. The undead defenders were clumsy; one skeleton attempted to strike Vlad but missed so badly it snapped its own arm. Arthur joined the fray with a magically infused bonk from his staff, and Alejandro ended the skirmish by firing a bullet into the last standing skeleton.

As the dust settled, the party turned to looting. They divided 6 gold pieces and 12 silver pieces, but the real prize was found by Lucas. Hidden in a secret compartment of a skeletal archer’s quiver, he discovered a single, exceptionally well-made arrow that seemed to hum with potential. With their pockets slightly heavier and the entrance secured, the weary adventurers decided to pause for the night, knowing the secrets of Ashardalon waited deeper below.

OPEN PLOT POINTS

  • The Identity of Ashardalon: A hidden inscription in Draconic was found behind a goblin corpse. Vlad identified this as the name of an ancient red dragon who once had a cult in this location. Importance: This suggests the citadel’s origins and the types of magic or guardians that may remain.
  • The “Organised” Goblins: A merchant reported seeing goblins carrying crates in an unusually disciplined manner. Importance: This contradicts the typical perception of goblins and suggests they may be serving a higher power (potentially the Outcast).
  • The Soul of the Rat: Arthur observed the soul of a slain rat wink at him before disappearing. Importance: This implies an unusual spiritual or magical thinning of the veil within the ruins.
  • “Wake It Not”: Draven’s sickle provided a cryptic warning about something resting “below” that must not be awakened. Importance: This serves as a warning of a potentially catastrophic threat deeper in the dungeon.
  • The 4-Month Gap: The goblin corpses in the entrance are roughly four months old, which aligns with when the previous adventuring party disappeared. Importance: Confirms the party is on the right trail but that the missing group likely moved deeper or met their end long ago.
