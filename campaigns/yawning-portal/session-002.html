SUMMARY

The session begins in the dark, silent sanctuary of the Sunless Citadel. The party of level two adventurers—the dragonborn paladin Vlad Laurent, tiefling warlock Draven Shadowveil, human druid Arthur Blackthorn, and wood-elf rogue Lucas Aedansbane—gather their bearings after defeating a group of skeletons. (Their companion Alejandro Perez, a forge cleric, is currently absent). As they look around the room, the Dungeon Master the DM notes that there are three doors: the entrance they came through and two other exits. Draven immediately notices that his magical sickle is vibrating, pointing toward both of the unexplored doors.

Vlad decides to investigate the door to the north first. Describing his state of mind as a mix of caution and the “doom-like feeling” of being called into a closed room by a parent, Vlad pokes the “ratty” door with a stick to check if it is locked. Finding no lock, he thrashes the handle and pushes the door open. The DM describes the room as a ruined, completely empty chamber filled with unstable walls and crumbling stone debris. Lucas decides to test the empty room by tossing a metal ball bearing inside; it makes a hollow, metallic click-clack against the stone floor, confirming there are no hidden pitfalls or secrets.

Meanwhile, Draven is drawn to the southern exit, where his magical sickle is throbbing intensely. Approaching the door, Vlad shines his torch to reveal a magnificent, twelve-foot-tall white marble statue of a dragon fashioned into a door. The DM notes that the door is reinforced by a mechanical lock and an “arcane lock”. Arthur steps forward and casts Detect Magic as a ritual, confirming the magical seals and noting that while a key must exist somewhere, they have no idea where it is. Vlad attempts to check for draconic kinship with his paladin magic, rolling an Arcanum check with disadvantage and Guidance from Arthur, but his roll of 9 reveals nothing. Draven searches the door for physical mechanisms, rolling a 7 on his Investigation check, which reveals a complex mechanical lock hidden behind the dragon’s imagery.

Lucas decides to try his hand at unlocking the door. He carefully inspects the keyhole for traps and, finding none, inserts his thieves’ tools. On his first attempt, he rolls a 13; the lock resists, and his lockpick snaps off inside the mechanism, raising the difficulty of any subsequent attempts. Arthur quickly places a hand on Lucas’s shoulder and casts Guidance (+1d4). Lucas carefully slides his tools back into the jammed lock and rolls a 17 (10 + 4 dexterity + 3 guidance), successfully picking the lock and swinging the door open into a dark hallway.

Before proceeding, the adventurers prepare their light sources. Arthur sheathes his quarterstaff, equips his wooden shield, and casts Druidcraft to light a torch. Lucas retrieves a mountable lantern from his Burglar’s Pack and latches it to his belt, while Vlad also lights a torch, leaving the party with three distinct light sources to pierce the darkness of the citadel. Seeking to scout the hallway, Lucas dips a metal ball bearing in oil, lights it with his lantern, and rolls it diagonally down the corridor. As the flaming ball rolls into a pile of debris, the party suddenly hears a faint, menacing chittering. Vlad immediately recognizes it as the skittering of giant rats.

Arthur steps south toward the debris field to investigate the noise, only for a giant rat to leap out from the shadows and ambush him. The DM calls for initiative, and Arthur rolls an 18, allowing him to act first. Rather than attacking immediately, Arthur tries a non-aggressive approach, casting Shillelagh (“Chilela” / “Shalala”) on his torch and attempting to shoo the rat away with the flame. The giant rat remains entirely unfazed by the fire. On its turn, the rat lunges and attempts to bite Arthur, but misses. In the next round, Arthur reaches into his rations and tosses a blueberry behind the rat to distract and befriend it. The rat ignores the berry, clearly more interested in Arthur’s fire. The DM reminds Arthur that as a druid, he knows giant rats are typically territorial but not major threats. Resigned to the fight, Arthur swings his magically infused torch and hits the rat, turning the creature into a “burnt crisp”. Arthur laments the loss of a potential friend whom he had hoped to ask about the key, naming the dead rat “Timothy”. Lucas, unimpressed, pours a few drops of oil on Timothy’s smoldering carcass and lights it on fire again.

While Arthur was dealing with the rat, Vlad and Lucas focused on the white marble dragon door in the hallway. Impatient with technical locks, Vlad decides to use a salvaged skeleton’s shield to bash the marble door down. With “passion” and help from Draven (+2 to his Strength check), Vlad rolls a 23. The skeletal shield shatters completely in Vlad’s hands, and while his immense blow puts a visible crack down the chest of the white marble dragon, the solid door remains firmly closed. Meanwhile, Lucas attempts to extract the broken lockpick head from the door’s keyhole using pliers from his thieves’ tools. The DM grants him advantage because Draven is assisting with the pliers. Lucas rolls a spectacular “natural 20 plus 1” dexterity check and successfully pulls the broken pick out. The DM declares the door is fully locked again, but the DC is now slightly lowered because the keyhole is clear. Vlad tries one last desperate maneuver, jumping up to grab the dragon statue’s horns to break them, but he only manages to chip the end of one horn before falling flat on his back.

Realizing they are at a dead end without a key, the party decides to backtrack and seek another route. The DM reminds Vlad of his personal quest to rescue his paladin acquaintance, Sir Braford, who has been missing for two months. Vlad casually remarks that Sir Braford can wait, and the party continues their backtracking. They return to the main entry corridor and see three doors: two are unlocked, and one is locked with a heavy padlock. Vlad approaches the padlocked door, which features a stone relief depicting “dragon-like fish swimming”. Rolling a Strength check of 22, Vlad smashes the padlock completely off, unlocking the door and allowing them to enter a cold, damp boiler room.

Inside, they find a large, rusted boiler that is currently offline due to a disconnected pipe. Vlad uses his strength to yank the top off the boiler’s tanker on a Strength check of 22. A torrent of water spills onto the floor, and two hostile elementals emerge: an Ice Mephit and a Steam Mephit. Initiative is rolled, with Draven going first at 19, followed by Vlad at 16, and Lucas joining the battle next round.

Draven opens the combat by casting Eldritch Blast on the “ice mephit” for 7 damage, and uses a spell slot to place a Hex on it. Lucas joins the fray, delivering a swift sneak attack against the un-blurred “ice mephit”, rolling a 16 to hit and dealing 12 total damage before using his Cunning Action to disengage and retreat. Next, Draven attacks the “ice mephit” with his pact blade, hitting and dealing 6 damage. The “ice mephit” immediately explodes in a shattering of ice shards (its Death Burst). While Draven’s class feature allows him to regain 5 hit points upon a kill, the 5 damage from the explosion nullifies the healing.

As the “ice mephit” dies, the “steam mephit” screams in Primordial that they have “killed my lover”. Arthur, who understands Primordial, tries to de-escalate the situation: “Greetings and salutations… we don’t mean to interrupt… we can easily just leave.” The “steam mephit” refuses to parley, declaring they want no peace. Because Arthur used his entire action to talk, he cannot attack this turn. The “steam mephit” attacks Draven, dealing 5 claw damage, and then activates its Blurred Form to make itself blurry and impose disadvantage on physical attacks.

On his turn, Vlad attacks the “steam mephit” with disadvantage. Despite the blur, Vlad lands his blow with a 15 to hit, dealing 13 damage and killing the elemental. As the “steam mephit” dies, it triggers a fiery explosion, dealing 5 fire damage to Vlad and Arthur. Both fail their Dexterity saving throws, with Vlad rolling an 8 and Arthur rolling a 5.

With the enemies defeated, Vlad heals himself for 5 hit points using his “Lay of Touch” (Lay on Hands). Draven jokingly remarks that they just witnessed Vlad “touch himself and heal himself.” Arthur is left badly wounded at 7 hit points.
